# Notes on `bench/b02/passage.md`

Domain: an invented field reading upland route-marker cairns as a two-register signalling system. Not AI, computing, forecasting or performance; shares no terms with `undercraft`.

## Discrimination pairs

1. **Laid sense vs worn sense.** Laid sense is in the body courses, is set by the builder and says where the route goes. Worn sense is carried by the tithe stone alone, is updated by passers and says what state the route is in (the ford). The passage names the confusion in both directions.
2. **Lean vs cant.** Lean is the whole cairn's tilt, from frost heave, and means nothing. Cant is a deliberate per-course offset measured against the course below, and gives direction. Lean does not change cant, but it misleads people who read cant by eye.
3. **Blind cairn vs dumb cairn.** Blind: canted courses, no tithe stone (laid sense only). Dumb: tithe stone, no cant (worn sense only). The names are easy to swap.
4. **Tessaly shadowing vs Orne inversion.** Shadowing: experienced readers, a conflict between registers, they follow laid sense. Inversion: strangers, body courses only, they read a cant-break as the direction.
5. (Minor) **Cant-break vs a meaningful reversal.** A single reversed course between agreeing ones marks a junction ahead, not the direction.

## Thin renamings of something real, stated honestly

- Stone cairns as waymarks are real, and so is the general idea of adding a stone as you pass. The two-register reading, cant, the tithe stone convention and everything measured are invented.
- Laid vs worn loosely echoes fixed signage vs temporary notices, or an authored text vs readers' marginal notes. A reader reasoning from that would guess "trust the fresher notice", which the passage makes right on the facts (C4) and wrong on behaviour (C3). So the echo gives a reader no net direction.
- The concord index is structurally a lag-1 agreement rate. Nothing about its *definition* (footing excluded, crown included) follows from that, and those are the load-bearing parts.
- The traffic inverse U (C6) and the tithe-weight inverse U (C7) are the familiar "middle is best" shapes. They were chosen on purpose to balance two claims where a middle-peak prediction is false (C1, C2). They are not balanced in isolation.
