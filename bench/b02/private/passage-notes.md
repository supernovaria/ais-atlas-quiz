## Discrimination pairs, and what each name gives away

1. **Dorran sense vs lisk sense.** Dorran: in the courses, set by the builder, says where the route goes. Lisk: carried by the pell stone alone, updated by passers, says what state the ford is in. Both are shepherds' words with no English sense; neither name points at builder or passer. "Pell stone" is also neutral. Residual leak: a stem that says "the register passers update" plus "the loose stone" answers itself from the description, not the name.
2. **Lean vs cant.** Lean: whole-cairn tilt, frost heave, meaningless. Cant: deliberate per-course offset (body course or crown) against the course below; gives direction. The name "lean" does give away that it is the whole-cairn tilt, and "cant" is a real word for tilt too, so a reader cannot use the names to tell which one is deliberate. What the names give away: that lean is the tilt of the whole; not which is meaningful.
3. **Hessle cairn vs Garth cairn.** Hessle: canted courses, no pell stone (dorran only). Garth: pell stone, no deliberate cant in body courses or crown (lisk only). Named after valleys; nothing given away. (The old "dumb" had a faint pull toward "no builder's message"; gone.)
4. **Tessaly effect vs Orne effect.** Tessaly: experienced readers, a register conflict, they follow the courses; a sound habit. Orne: strangers, courses only, they read a hob as the direction; a mistake. Neutral names. Residual leak: the Tessaly effect sits in Tessaly's survey of locals, so a reader who remembers only "Tessaly surveyed locals" could guess its population.
5. **Marhaug's index vs Tessaly's index.** Marhaug's counts the crown; Tessaly's, the current standard since 1986, leaves it out. The names give away which is which only to a reader told that Tessaly objected to the crown (P4), in which case "Tessaly's index drops the crown" follows.
6. **Stale pell stone vs hask cairn: a pair that seems distinct and is one thing** (C22). Older reports list them as two faults; Tessaly showed the hask cairn (stone bedded into the crown) is a stone left unturned on a quiet route, i.e. a stale one. "Hask" is neutral. Tagged guessable (conservative): "a bedded stone is one left unturned" is close to physical intuition.

The old pair 5 ("cant-break vs a meaningful reversal") is dropped: it had no second member and duplicated the Orne effect.

## Thin renamings of something real, stated honestly

- Stone cairns as waymarks are real, and so is the idea of adding a stone as you pass. The two-register reading, cant, the pell stone convention and everything measured are invented.
- Dorran vs lisk loosely echoes fixed signage vs temporary notices. A reader reasoning "trust the fresher notice" is now wrong on both behaviour-and-outcome claims taken together: locals follow the fixed courses (C3, guessable) and are right to (C4, the fresher stone was usually wrong).
- **Whole frame: the folk theory of expertise** ("experts are better overall but stuck in their habits"; cf. expertise reversal, Einstellung). Before revision it predicted all five locals-vs-strangers claims. It is now tagged and counted: right on C1, C3, C5, C11, C21; wrong on C4 (the locals' habit is sound) and C19 (locals do *better* on inconsistent cairns). 5 right, 2 wrong (71%, under the 75% warning line). Its plainer mirror, **"experts beat beginners"**, is tagged separately: right on C4, C11, C19; wrong on C5 (lean misleads locals more) and C21 (strangers misroute less at leaning Garth cairns). 3 right, 2 wrong. The reviewer's suggested fix (C19 "slightly better" → "no better") was not used: it would make C19's naive answer ("high-concord cairns at least as easy for locals") right and push the share over 70%. C21 was added instead; it also feeds the habit-bound frame, which is why that frame rose to 5/2.
- **C13/C14 echo the Ruskin/Morris case against Viollet-le-Duc-style restoration** (the restored work is tidier and easier to read, and says less). Both are tagged naive-right for that reason.
- The concord index is structurally a lag-1 agreement rate. Its definition's load-bearing parts are the footing and crown rules, now tagged guessable (P1) and practice-dependent (C18).
- C6 (traffic, peaked) is the familiar "middle is best" shape. C7 (pell-stone weight) was changed from peaked to rising and moved to a different section, so the two are no longer a matched pair.
