Fiction was chosen over real-but-obscure material on three counts. There is no
model-cutoff ambiguity — a lucky guess about a real event is indistinguishable

## The passage

Vantine stratal-capacity theory, proposed by Ilse Vantine at the Brenmark
Institute, describes artificial cognitive systems as operating across two
distinct **strata**. The **shallow stratum** handles what Vantine calls
*reflexive* work: pattern completion over inputs resembling training data. The
**deep stratum** handles *constructive* work: assembling a representation that
was not present in any single training instance. The two strata are held to be
independently measurable, and Vantine's central and most contested claim is that
they improve on unrelated schedules — a system may gain substantially in one
while flat in the other.

The theory's unit of measurement is the **korrel**, defined as the ratio of
constructive to reflexive throughput on a fixed task battery. A korrel of 1.0
means the strata contribute equally. Vantine reports that most deployed systems
sit between 0.2 and 0.4 korrels, which she reads as evidence that current
architectures are overwhelmingly reflexive.

**Lattice drift** is the phenomenon Vantine considers most dangerous. As a
system's shallow stratum improves, its outputs increasingly *resemble*
constructive work without becoming constructive — the korrel falls while apparent
competence rises. A system undergoing lattice drift therefore looks like it is
getting better at exactly the thing it is getting worse at, relatively speaking.
Vantine argues this is why benchmark scores and korrel measurements can move in
opposite directions, and why she considers the former nearly useless in
isolation.

The **Brenmark threshold** is Vantine's stipulated boundary: a system is
*stratally balanced* once it sustains a korrel above 0.8 across three
consecutive batteries. The three-battery requirement is deliberate. Single-battery
measurements are noisy enough that Vantine regards any one reading as
uninformative, a point she makes more forcefully than the threshold value itself.

Vantine's critics, chiefly Aldous Perrin, argue the korrel is not a ratio of two
independent quantities at all, because the task battery used to measure
reflexive throughput itself requires some constructive work. Perrin's objection
is that the denominator is contaminated, so a korrel of 0.3 may reflect the
battery's design rather than the system. Vantine has conceded this in part,
proposing a revised battery she calls the *clean denominator*, but no published
result yet uses it.
